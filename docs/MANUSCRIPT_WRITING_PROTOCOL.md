WOS-Q1 / VERY STRONG PUBLICATION POTENTIAL TARGET



BENCHMARK ARTICLE:

“Tournament Design as Climate Governance:

Scope 3 Life-Cycle Emissions and Decarbonization

in the 2026 FIFA World Cup”



The benchmark is used for manuscript architecture, flow,

evidence discipline, table/figure integration, discussion structure,

and conclusion strength. Text or topic-specific content must not be copied.



============================================================

1\. TITLE

============================================================



The title must:

\- represent the entire manuscript accurately;

\- foreground the actual novelty;

\- be scientifically precise but editorially attractive;

\- avoid exaggerated “first-ever” claims;

\- reflect the same-BEV continue-versus-immediate-replacement problem;

\- preferably signal trajectory-free/state-conditioned analysis or the

&#x20; carbon-decision mechanism where this improves clarity;

\- be optimized for WoS-Q1 editor and reviewer interest.



Several candidate titles must be compared before the final title is locked.



============================================================

2\. ABSTRACT

============================================================



Target: 140–150 words.



The abstract must contain, in a compact continuous narrative:

1\. scientific problem;

2\. research gap;

3\. objective;

4\. methodological architecture;

5\. principal quantitative findings;

6\. principal mechanism;

7\. scientific implication/contribution.



The abstract must:

\- agree exactly with the title and manuscript;

\- contain every selected keyword naturally;

\- contain no unsupported novelty claim;

\- contain no result not shown in the manuscript;

\- avoid citations unless target-journal rules require them;

\- read as natural human academic prose rather than a template.



============================================================

3\. KEYWORDS

============================================================



Keywords must:

\- all appear naturally in the Abstract;

\- reflect the actual searchable scientific contribution;

\- avoid redundant duplication of title terms where possible;

\- support bibliometric discoverability.



Final keywords must be selected only after the title and abstract are stable.



============================================================

4\. INTRODUCTION

============================================================



Target: 900–1,000 words.



Mandatory order:



1\. Strong scientific hook

2\. Connection with relevant existing theory/conceptual reasoning

3\. State of the scientific problem

4\. Explicit literature gap

5\. Contribution to the field

6\. Research question(s)

7\. Scientific and practical importance

8\. Scope and boundary of the study

9\. Hypotheses / pre-specified analytical propositions



The Introduction must:

\- move from broad scientific problem to the exact research problem;

\- explain why existing degradation-aware LCA and retirement studies

&#x20; do not resolve the present problem;

\- position trajectory-free, state-conditioned, same-BEV

&#x20; continue-versus-immediate-replacement lifecycle-GHG identification

&#x20; as the core contribution;

\- distinguish headline novelty from supporting contributions;

\- avoid result leakage before the formal Results section.



IMPORTANT:

Hypotheses may only be presented as ex-ante hypotheses if they can be

traced to pre-result project decisions/preregistration.

No post-result hypothesis may be presented as though it had been

specified before analysis.

Where necessary, use “analytical proposition” or “pre-specified

expectation” instead.



============================================================

5\. LITERATURE REVIEW AND THEORETICAL FRAMEWORK

============================================================



Target: 1,200–1,300 words.



Scientific literature priority:

\- Web of Science;

\- preferably Q1 journals;

\- preferably 2020 onwards;

\- DOI verified individually;

\- recent literature given priority.



Older literature is permitted only for:

\- foundational theory;

\- foundational methodology;

\- indispensable seminal evidence.



Official datasets, government reports and technical documentation may be

used for data provenance even though they are not WoS-Q1 publications.



The review must be DISCUSSIVE, not bibliographic listing.



It must be divided into thematic domains, likely including:

\- EV battery degradation and multidimensional state of health;

\- degradation-to-vehicle-energy translation;

\- lifecycle GHG and battery replacement;

\- battery retirement and end-of-first-life decision literature;

\- prospective battery-manufacturing decarbonization;

\- temporal electricity-carbon accounting;

\- residual vehicle service and mobility requirements;

\- robust/partial-identification decision architectures.



Each thematic section must:

\- synthesize studies;

\- compare assumptions and boundaries;

\- identify contradictions;

\- state what remains unresolved;

\- connect directly to the manuscript RQ.



Citation-reference correspondence must be 100%.



Every DOI must be verified.



No fabricated reference is permitted.



============================================================

6\. MATERIALS AND METHODS

============================================================



Target: approximately 1,650–1,850 words.



The Methods section must faithfully document the validated scientific

pipeline. No fashionable method may be added merely to make the paper

appear more sophisticated.



The methodology must cover:

\- functional unit and system boundary;

\- same-BEV continue versus immediate battery replacement decision;

\- degradation-state evidence;

\- capacity SOH and resistance architecture;

\- 10-ms primary and 1-s alternative specification;

\- FASTSim vehicle-energy translation;

\- charging conversion;

\- vehicle survival/residual service;

\- temporal grid-carbon pathways;

\- prospective battery-manufacturing pathways;

\- trajectory-free set identification;

\- DeltaG accounting;

\- identified intervals;

\- classification rules;

\- structural robustness;

\- magnitude/break-even defense;

\- limitations and claim constraints.



All mathematical expressions must be in proper LaTeX mathematical form.



Every important equation must:

\- be numbered;

\- be referenced in the text;

\- define all symbols immediately after the equation;

\- use consistent notation throughout the paper.



No undefined symbol is permitted.



No resistance-percent-to-energy-percent proportional shortcut is permitted.



No unsupported degradation trajectory is permitted.



============================================================

7\. TABLES AND FIGURES

============================================================



Only scientifically necessary tables/figures are allowed.



Every table/figure must:

\- be publication quality;

\- have a complete title/caption;

\- define abbreviations;

\- state units;

\- state evidence/source where relevant;

\- distinguish primary, robustness and conditional/extreme evidence;

\- be referenced explicitly in the manuscript text;

\- be interpreted immediately after introduction in the text.



No table/figure may simply repeat prose.



Main-text figures should show the scientific mechanism rather than

decorative information.



Additional diagnostics may be moved to Appendices/Supplementary Material.



============================================================

8\. RESULTS

============================================================



Target: approximately 1,150–1,350 words.



Results must:

\- answer the research questions directly;

\- report quantitative results before interpretation;

\- separate primary results from robustness results;

\- show identified intervals, not only point estimates;

\- explicitly report the ROBUST CONTINUE result within its evidence domain;

\- present temporal results;

\- present mechanism decomposition;

\- report 10-ms / 1-s structural robustness;

\- report grid/LRMER/regional/context robustness;

\- disclose extreme/conditional stress separately.



Results must not:

\- overclaim engineering feasibility;

\- hide the aged-state power limitation;

\- claim Xu/recycling sensitivities were completed;

\- infer time-to-state;

\- perform or imply replacement-time optimization.



============================================================

9\. DISCUSSION

============================================================



Target: approximately 1,400–1,550 words.



Discussion must be thematic.



Each major theme should integrate:

1\. empirical/result finding;

2\. hypothesis/proposition or RQ;

3\. theoretical interpretation;

4\. mechanism;

5\. comparison with previous literature;

6\. scientific implication;

7\. managerial/policy/engineering implication where justified.



Headline discussion themes should prioritize:

\- trajectory-free state-conditioned carbon decision;

\- manufacturing-burden versus operational-saving asymmetry;

\- temporal decarbonization and persistence of the decision;

\- why multidimensional battery health remains scientifically relevant

&#x20; even when resistance does not reverse the classification;

\- robustness and partial-identification implications.



Dual-state resistance and identification leverage are supporting

contributions, not forced headline results.



Limitations must be explicit:

\- aged-state power capability not state-resolved;

\- Xu 2022 not numerically implemented in P16;

\- recycling-credit sensitivity not numerically implemented;

\- no trajectory/time-to-state inference.



============================================================

10\. CONCLUSION

============================================================



Target: 600–900 words.

Preferred working target: approximately 650–800 words.



Conclusion must:

\- answer the Primary RQ clearly;

\- synthesize the main quantitative finding;

\- explain the mechanism;

\- summarize the temporal and structural robustness;

\- state the scientific contribution;

\- state theoretical/methodological contribution;

\- explain practical implications only within supported bounds;

\- state central limitations;

\- provide future-research direction;

\- end with a strong conceptual closing statement.



The final paragraph must be memorable but scientifically disciplined.



No new result, citation-dependent argument or unsupported claim may be

introduced in the Conclusion.

Third-party raw data must not be redistributed when licences prohibit it.



The final manuscript must provide the verified GitHub URL.
